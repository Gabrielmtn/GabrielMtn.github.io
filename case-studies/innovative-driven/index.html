Innovative Driven

Product Architect · Supporting the software team and the VP of Engineering

Sector
eDiscovery / Legal tech
Systems
Salesforce · Review platform · Azure DevOps
Scope
Process mapping · Automation strategy · Epic and user stories
The fundamental
Make the process visible before you try to fix it.
Working principle

Innovative Driven builds eDiscovery software used by about 40 law firms. Every new firm has to be onboarded before its review can start. Once a deal closed in Salesforce, people across several teams set up the client, the matter, the review workspace, user access, and a way for the firm to send its data. That took weeks.

Each team knew its own steps, but nobody could see the whole process at once. My job was to give everyone one shared picture of it, lay the groundwork to change it, and then support the engineers while they built.

The map

Mapping every step and handoff.

I walked through onboarding with Client Services and the VP of Engineering and mapped every step in Figma: who did it, what they needed, and where the work waited for the next person. With every handoff in one place, the bottlenecks were easy to point at. The model below is a working version of that map. Switch between before and after, then play it.

Generalized from my process map, with no client data. The workspace setup uses Relativity's terms for the same parts: Client, Matter, workspace template, groups and permissions, processing profile, and custodians.

The work

From a map to a plan engineering could run with.

  1. Mapped the existing onboarding flow end to end in Figma with Client Services and the VP of Engineering, marking every handoff and bottleneck along the way.
  2. Brought the map into strategy meetings with stakeholders. We used it to decide how ambitious the first version should be and which steps to automate first.
  3. Scoped the work as an epic and wrote the user stories in Azure DevOps, so the team had a plan to start building from on day one. That included a notification telling the Client Manager to complete the onboarding form and assign the team.
  4. Handed the plan to the engineers, who built it and iterated on it. I kept the foundation solid along the way: requirements, scope, and answers when questions came up.
Outcome
